How the work usually starts

Most calls start the same way. Mortar joints have opened up, a chimney has started shedding brick, front steps have gone soft at the tread, or water is getting into a foundation wall. Masonry rarely fails all at once, so the question is normally how much has to come apart before the repair holds.

We look at the wall, tell you what we would do, and give you a price for it. There is no charge for the estimate, and no pressure attached to it.

The craft

What tuckpointing actually is

Mortar is the part that is supposed to fail

In a well built wall, the mortar is deliberately softer than the brick. It takes the movement, the moisture and the freeze and thaw cycles so the brick does not, and after enough decades of that it cracks and recedes from the face. That is the joint doing its job, right up until open joints start letting water behind the wall.

Tuckpointing, also called repointing, is the repair: rake the failed mortar out to sound depth, repack the joint with mortar matched to the wall, and tool it flush so the face sheds water again. Done properly it is the difference between maintaining a wall and eventually rebuilding one.
